Contributors and resume proof

Everyone who adds to this site gets credit that employers can check for themselves.

Why this works as resume proof

A line on a resume is a claim. A merged pull request is evidence. Every change to this site is recorded on GitHub with your name, the date, exactly what you changed, and who approved it. Anyone can open the link and see it. That is much stronger than "member of a club."

How to get credit, step by step

  1. Use your own GitHub account (free) and make your profile look professional: a real name or clear username, and a short bio.
  2. Make your change as a pull request, as described on the Start here page. Edits made on the GitHub website are automatically credited to your account.
  3. Add yourself to the table below in the same pull request, with a link to your work. You can use your GitHub handle only, or add your name if you want.
  4. Once it is merged, copy the link to your pull request. It never expires.
  5. Use the builder below to write your resume bullets and LinkedIn entry.
  6. Be honest about AI help. If an AI assistant helped, say "with AI assistance." Employers respect that, and they dislike overclaiming.

Contributor levels

Contributor

At least one merged pull request, such as a prompt, a meeting note, a glossary word or a fix.

Guide author

Wrote or substantially improved a full tool guide, including its business use case.

Maintainer

Reviews and merges pull requests, and keeps the site accurate.

These are club labels, not job titles. Describe your work in your own words and only claim what you did.
