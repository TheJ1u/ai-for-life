Using AI safely in a business

AI saves real time, but the mistakes are expensive: leaked client data, wrong facts, broken rules. This page is a practical checklist.

General guidance for learning. It is not legal advice. Rules differ by industry and change, so check with your compliance contact, your carrier or company policy, and an attorney when it matters.

Can I paste this into an AI tool?

The seven rules

  1. Protect private data. Treat anything you paste as leaving your control until you know the tool's data policy. Personal and client information, health details, financial identifiers and credentials should stay out unless your organization has approved the tool for that use.
  2. Know the tool's data settings. Check whether your chats can be used to train models, how long they are kept, and whether a business plan gives stronger protection. Policies change, so read the current one.
  3. Verify before you trust. AI can state wrong facts with total confidence (a hallucination). Check names, numbers, dates, prices and legal or medical claims against an official source.
  4. Keep a human responsible. A person should review anything that goes to a customer, regulator or the public. "The AI wrote it" is not a defense.
  5. Be honest about AI. Follow each platform's rules for labeling AI-made content. Never present an AI video as a real testimonial, a real event or a real person.
  6. Respect other people's rights. Do not recreate a real person's face or voice without permission. Use only music, images and text you have the right to use, and summarize instead of copying.
  7. Follow the industry rules. Advertising and marketing rules apply no matter how the content was made. In regulated fields such as insurance, health and finance, get content approved the same way you would for anything else.

Examples by situation

Insurance agent

Fine: ask AI to explain a general concept in plain language. Not fine: paste a client's name, health conditions or policy numbers. Medicare and other plan marketing is regulated, so run any AI-written ad or script through your normal approval process.

Marketing team

Fine: draft ad ideas and storyboards. Check every claim is true and provable, label AI content where required, and never show a fake customer as if it were real.

Student

Fine: use AI to explain, quiz and brainstorm. Check your school's policy on disclosure, never submit AI text as your own if rules forbid it, and verify every fact and source it gives you.

Before you publish or send: a 60-second check

  • No private or client information is in it.
  • I checked every fact, number and name against a real source.
  • I own or have rights to every image, clip, voice and song.
  • AI use is labeled where the platform or my field requires it.
  • The claims would hold up if someone challenged them.
  • The right person approved it, if my field needs approval.

Why it matters for business

Data privacy, accuracy and accountability are the same questions an IT or risk team asks about any system. Companies are writing AI policies now, and knowing the basics makes you more useful on day one.
