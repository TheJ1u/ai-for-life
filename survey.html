AI 101 feedback survey

Measure whether a session worked: copy these questions into a free form and compare confidence before and after.

This site does not collect any answers. It is a static page with no database. Use the questions below to build a free form in Google Forms or Microsoft Forms, or print them. That way you control the data and can tell people exactly who sees it.

How to set it up in five minutes

  1. Create a new form in Google Forms or Microsoft Forms (both have free versions).
  2. Copy a part below and paste the questions in. Use "linear scale 1 to 5" for the rating questions and "multiple choice" or "short answer" for the rest.
  3. Turn on anonymous responses. Do not ask for names, emails or other personal details unless you need them.
  4. Put the form link in a QR code from the Share page or shorten it, and show it at the end of the session.
  5. Open the responses sheet after the session and compare Part A with Part B.

Part A: before the session (1 minute)

  1. How confident are you using AI tools right now? (1 = not at all, 5 = very confident)
  2. How often do you use AI tools? (Never / A few times a year / Monthly / Weekly / Daily)
  3. What do you most want to use AI for? (Open answer)

  7. What should we change or add next time? (Open answer)
  8. Would you recommend this session to a friend? (Yes / Maybe / No)
  9. May we share your anonymous answers to improve the club? (Yes / No)

Optional: five-question knowledge check

This matches the questions at the end of AI 101. Make them multiple choice and mark the correct answer so the form can score them.

  1. What should you do before using an AI-generated statistic in a client email?
    Answer: Check it against an official or original source
  2. Which is safest to paste into a free AI chat tool?
    Answer: Your own draft paragraph with no private details
  3. A prompt is...
    Answer: The instruction and context you give the AI
  4. Who is responsible for a false claim the AI wrote in your ad?
    Answer: You and your business
  5. AI is usually best at...
    Answer: First drafts, summaries and brainstorms that a person reviews

How to read the results

  • Confidence change: subtract each person's Part A confidence from Part B. Look at the average change, not just the final score.
  • Understanding questions: a result of 4 or higher on most is a good sign. Low scores on "what not to paste" mean spend more time on safety next time.
  • Knowledge check: if most people miss the same question, reteach that idea.
  • Open answers: group them into themes and pick one change per session.
  • Small groups: with fewer than about 15 responses, treat the numbers as hints, not proof.

Privacy and fairness

  • Say who will see the answers and how they will be used.
  • Keep it anonymous and optional.
  • Do not ask about health, finances, school grades or other sensitive details.
  • Share results in groups only, never one person's answers.
