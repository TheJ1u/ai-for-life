Build Your Own Model

A beginner path from using AI tools to training and publishing a small model of your own.

By Club guide

What it is

You do not need to train a giant model from scratch (that costs millions). The realistic path is to fine-tune an existing open model on your own data, or to build a small classic machine-learning model for a business problem. This page shows the ladder from easiest to hardest.

Why people use it

  • Shows employers you understand how AI works, not just how to use it.
  • A model trained on your data can beat a general tool for a narrow task.
  • Great portfolio project for Information Systems, analytics and startups.

How to get started

  1. Level 1, use: run an existing model on Hugging Face (3-line pipeline).
  2. Level 2, prompt: give a model examples and instructions (no training).
  3. Level 3, fine-tune: adapt an open model with your own examples. LoRA is a cheap way to do this.
  4. Level 4, classic ML: train a small model with scikit-learn on a spreadsheet (for example, predicting which leads convert).
  5. Publish: upload your model and a model card to Hugging Face and link it in your portfolio.

from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression

Why it matters for business and IS

Data comes first. Clean, labeled data is usually the hard part and the real asset. Never train on private client data without permission.

Quick facts

  • Free tools: Google Colab, Hugging Face, scikit-learn, Kaggle datasets.
  • Fine-tuning needs a GPU; Colab's free tier can handle small experiments.
  • Measure results with held-out test data, not the data you trained on.

What to learn next

Complete Kaggle's free 'Intro to Machine Learning', then replicate it with your own small dataset.
