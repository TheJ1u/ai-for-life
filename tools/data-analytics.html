Data analytics

Check the numbers before the story

Use six synthetic rows to catch duplicates, missing prices, and conclusions the data cannot support. These are invented practice orders, not club or business results.

What it is

Data analytics turns records into evidence for a decision. Cleaning means identifying errors and deciding how to handle them before calculating totals.

How to get started

Use any of our ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini guides. Basic text chat is enough; paid uploads are optional. Download the synthetic CSV or copy it below.

Try this

Analyze the synthetic CSV above. First identify duplicate IDs, negative quantities, and missing prices. Do not silently change them. Exclude ambiguous rows A104 and A105 from a provisional total; count A103 once. Show each included line total, total units, revenue, and exclusions. Explain why this sample cannot establish profit, demand, or a trend.

Full walkthrough

  1. Count six input rows. A103 appears twice with identical values; confirm duplication before removing records in real work.
  2. Flag A104: a negative quantity might be a return or an error. Ask the data owner. A105 has no price; never treat missing as zero.
  3. For this exercise only, count A101, A102 and A103 once. Exclude A104 and A105 pending clarification.
  4. Calculate independently: 2 × $5 = $10; 3 × $2 = $6; 1 × $5 = $5. Total: 6 units and $21 across 3 orders.
  5. Compare the AI answer line by line. A $26 total likely counted A103 twice. Save both original and cleaned data with an explanation of every change.

Check your work

There are 3 included rows and 3 excluded rows (one duplicate and two unresolved records). Revenue is not profit: costs are missing. No dates or representative sample exist, so do not claim growth or forecast demand.

Common problems

If the AI fills a missing price, tell it to keep the value unknown. If it treats the negative quantity as an error, ask what evidence would distinguish a return. Do not accept a chart until its totals reconcile with your calculation.

Business use case

A retailer checks an export before ordering inventory. A person resolves exceptions with the source system, compares verified totals, and approves the report. Measure correction rates and review time; one mistaken inventory order can outweigh the time saved. Keep customer data out of public AI tools.
