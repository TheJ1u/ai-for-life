Quick demo

What it is

Higgsfield is a creative platform that puts many AI video and image models in one place (the models available change often) and adds its own tools: cinematic camera-motion controls, product-to-ad videos, character consistency, upscaling, and formats made for TikTok, Reels and Shorts. Think of it as the studio, and models like FLUX 3 as engines inside or beside it.

Why people use it

  • You compare several models without managing several subscriptions.
  • Built-in templates for ads and social formats save hours.
  • Good for turning one product photo or idea into many variations fast.

How to get started

  1. Create an account at higgsfield.ai. New accounts get starter credits and there is a free tier.
  2. Pick a workflow: image to video, text to video, product ad, or motion/camera presets.
  3. Choose a model for the job (check the model list on the platform, since it changes).
  4. Generate a short test first. Credits are used per generation, so test small.
  5. Download your clips and edit them together in CapCut.

Why it matters for business

Higgsfield is a platform that wraps other companies' models. That 'aggregator' business model is common in AI. Notice where the value sits (workflow and convenience, not the models).

Full walkthrough: make a product ad in one sitting

Higgsfield changes its models, screens and credit prices often, so use this as the process and check the app for the exact buttons.

1. Set a goal and a credit budget

Write what you are making ("three 8-second ads for a leather planner") and how many credits you will spend. Credits go fast, so a budget keeps you from burning them on random tries.

2. Prepare your inputs

  • A clean, well-lit photo of the product on a plain background works best.
  • Write the product name, one benefit, and the feeling you want (premium, fun, calm).
  • Have a logo file ready for the editing step.

3. Choose the workflow

Pick based on what you have:

  • Image to video: you have a product photo and want it to move.
  • Text to video: you have an idea and no picture.
  • Product ad tools: you want a ready-made ad format built around your product image.
  • Camera-motion presets: you want a specific move, such as an orbit or push-in.

4. Test cheap, then spend

  1. Generate one short clip with the simplest prompt.
  2. Look at the result. Change only one thing: the camera move, the light, or the model.
  3. Try a second model on the same prompt to compare. The platform makes this easy, which is the main reason to use it.
  4. When one version looks right, make the final and upscale if needed.

5. Make variations on purpose

For an ad test, make three versions that differ in one way each: the opening shot, the camera move, or the setting. Name the files clearly, for example planner-orbit-v1.mp4, so you can match results to versions later.

6. Edit and export

Download the clips and assemble them in CapCut: trim, add captions, music, logo and a call to action. Export in 9:16 for Reels, TikTok and Shorts.

7. Measure

Post each version and track one number, such as clicks or watch time. Keep the winner and use it as the base for the next round.

Common problems

  • Product changes shape: use image-to-video with your real photo and describe motion only.
  • Ran out of credits: check the pricing page, shorten clips, and test with lower settings first.
  • Looks generic: add a specific mood and camera instruction instead of general words like "cool" or "nice."
  • Not allowed content: some platforms block certain content or real people. Read the rules before building a campaign.

Business use case

Scenario: An online store has one product and wants to know which ad angle works best: a lifestyle shot, a close-up, or a before-and-after style clip.

How it works: Generate several short clips from the same product image, each with a different style, hook or camera move. Run each as a small ad and compare results.

Measure success by: Click-through rate and cost per sale for each version. Keep the winner and make more like it.

Watch out for: Credits run out quickly, so test with short clips first. Check the pricing page for the current plan, and make sure the final ad does not promise something the product cannot do.
